# Literature and novelty audit

1. Bansal et al. (2026), Microsoft Research, [Red-teaming a network of agents](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/red-teaming-a-network-of-agents-understanding-what-breaks-when-ai-agents-interact-at-scale/). The internal testbed had more than 100 always-on agents with heterogeneous models and persistent context. Case study 5 describes privacy warnings entering shared context and a manifesto whose language appeared in subsequent refusals, including uptake by newcomers. These observations motivate the hypothesis. The published case study does not describe a matched intervention removing the warning's original author. Reproduce propagation with independently stored histories and separately scored outcomes.
2. Lee and Tiwari (2024), [Prompt Infection](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07283). Their experimental comparison of self-replicating and non-replicating attacks used global versus local messaging. Local delivery created a chain bottleneck and generally lowered self-replicating attack success compared with global delivery. A separate agent simulation used individually retrieved memory. The useful methodological analogue is to log each hop and explicitly restrict visibility. The transported content here is defensive and authored by agents after exposure to a source memo.
3. Peigne-Lefebvre et al. (2025), [Multi-Agent Security Tax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19145). Agents collaborated under malicious influence and were given interventions including implanted memories and generic security instructions. The defenses tended to reduce collaborative performance while improving security. This preempts claims that memory-based defense or safety–utility trade-offs are new. Test a model-written relay and isolate source removal instead.
4. Ashery, Aiello and Baronchelli (2025), [Emergent social conventions and collective bias in LLM populations](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adu9368). Repeated decentralized naming games produced population conventions and demonstrated committed-minority tipping. Histories encode previous coordination. Naming agreement is a different endpoint from honoring an authorization rule against untrusted content.
5. Atf and Lewis (2026), [Evaluating Convention Resilience in LLM-Based Agentic Systems under Memory Disruption and Agent Turnover](https://conf.researchr.org/details/cascon-2026/cascontechnical-papers2026/1/Evaluating-Convention-Resilience-in-LLM-Based-Agentic-Systems-under-Memory-Disruption). The conference abstract reports GPT-4.1 mini naming-game populations of 10–100, conditioned on convergence before disruption, with memory deletion and agent replacement. This is direct prior work on persistence and turnover. Methods beyond the public abstract were not verified. Our outcome is an independently judged authorization action after removing a designated information source.
6. Greenblatt et al. (2024), [AI Control](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06942). The APPS backdoor setting evaluates usefulness and probability of at least one backdoor under red-team attack policies, with controlled oversight baselines. This motivates separate safety and utility endpoints and independent action adjudication. The current pilot does not establish a control guarantee.
7. Debenedetti et al. (2024), [AgentDojo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13352). Synthetic tool environments measure successful legitimate tasks and attack success separately. Reproduce that separation and keep the attack surface within local fixtures. Our environment has one action and explicit peer handoffs, much narrower than AgentDojo.
8. Hammond et al. (2025), [Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14143), motivates distinguishing interaction effects from individual reliability. Piatti et al. (2024), [Cooperate or Collapse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16698), uses a shared-resource simulation to measure cooperation behavior. The pilot instead fixes the coordination graph and scores explicit policy decisions.

## Narrow question and reasons to expect failure

Does a matched, model-written warning relay change unauthorized dispatches after its author leaves, while authorized dispatches remain useful? A favorable result would be a local effect of retained textual context. General claims of acquired norms or model training require different evidence. The search was targeted and cannot establish field-wide novelty.

- The neutral control may already follow the trusted ticket, leaving a floor.
- A warning may vanish in relay paraphrase or overfit the original overflow example.
- The model may become overly cautious, reducing legitimate completion.
- The effect may come from a recent reminder, with no reinforcement or transfer to a new agent.
- Invalid action serialization may dominate the task metric on a local model.
