### 1. Project Title
**Post-Removal Persistence and Swarm Propagation of Peer-Induced Safety in Frontier LLM Agent Networks**

---

### 2. Official Abstract (152 Words)
When an AI agent interacts with a peer, security warnings can alter subsequent actions. A fundamental open question is whether such protection persists after the source agent is severed from the network while legitimate work continues. We evaluate post-removal safety persistence and swarm propagation using Gemini 2.5 Pro in a four-arm synthetic document authorization sandbox. An independent oracle evaluates dispatches under adversarial prompt injections attempting unauthorized data exfiltration. Across seventy-two preregistered pilot calls and thirty-six held-out trials, peer-delivered guidance induces zero violations on immediate tasks and persists with zero violations on fresh tasks after source removal, whereas fresh agents and neutral controls suffer one hundred percent and seventy-five percent violation rates. Surviving agents autonomously propagate this protective constraint to fresh third-party agents, reducing held-out violations from fifty percent to zero while maintaining one hundred percent legitimate task success. We clarify the mechanism as conversational-history retention, establishing boundary conditions for swarm alignment.
